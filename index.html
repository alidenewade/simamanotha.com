Research

Pricing Real Estate Using Machine Learning & Time Series Models
Honours project

Machine learning prices property well but treats every transaction as independent, discarding the market cycles and persistence that housing markets are built on. This study tests whether a hybrid framework fixes that: four models — multiple linear regression, random forest, support vector machine and gradient boosting — fitted to twenty years of one-family dwelling transactions in Brooklyn, New York City, then paired with an ARIMA model applied to their residuals.

Hybridisation improved every model. Prediction error fell by roughly 35% and average R² rose from 0.37 to 0.63, with Diebold–Mariano tests confirming the gains were statistically significant. The residuals carry real temporal information that the machine learning models threw away — and the same logic applies wherever cross-sectional risk factors and shifting market conditions both drive the answer, premium rating included.
